# Video 4 — The Display Process

## MODULE 3 — THE DISPLAY PROCESS

### 3.1 Why this process exists
This process makes sure that what the customer *sees* on the shelf matches company standards — correct arrangement, no unsaleable stock left in view, correct placement tier, and a documented photo trail proving compliance.

### 3.2 Step-by-step walkthrough

**Step 1 — Display Setup → Decision: "Planogram (VM Guidelines) Available?"**
A **planogram** is the visual merchandising (VM) blueprint — the exact map of what goes where on a given store's shelving.

- **No** → **Contact NPD / Design Team for planogram** → **Updated Planogram received** → **Share with Store partner for approval** → **Implementation by AM** → **Follow Planogram** (rejoins the main flow)
- **Yes** → **Display Execution** (arrange product as per the planogram)

> 🖼️ A **sample planogram** is embedded directly in the chart at this point — a real shelf-map image — so trainees can see exactly what a finished, compliant display should look like.

> 💡 **Important nuance from the meeting:** planograms are **not identical across all stores.** They vary based on each store's available physical infrastructure (number of walls, shelf types, etc.) and the store's tier — stores are bucketed into **High, Medium, and Low throughput** categories, and each category/infra combination has its own reference planogram. A brand-new product (NPD) doesn't get an entirely new planogram; instead it's slotted into its existing product group/category and given elevated visibility (eye-level or focal placement) until its sales performance is tracked and it earns a bigger footprint.

**Step 2 — Decision: "Display Setup Complete?"**
- **No** → **Re-arrange Display (According to planogram)** → loops back into Display Execution
- **Yes** → **Move to visual check**

**Step 3 — Quality Check → Decision: "Any Non-saleable plants?"**
> 🟡 **Sticky notes attached here:**
> - *Visual check guidelines:* (1) products as per specs, (2) as per planogram, placed at category level, (3) is placement too cluttered or too distanced?
> - *Link with Specs sheet, add feedback form link for QC issues*

**Step 3a — If "Yes" (non-saleable plants found):**
- **Move to back of shelf** *(cadence: within 1 day)*
- **Check by supervisor**
- **Decision: "Is Repair Possible?"**
  - **Yes** → **Reshelf**, following a 3-step process: **1. Prune → 2. Clean → 3. Shine Spray**
  - **No** → **Mark for RTV** *(RTV = Return to Vendor/Warehouse)* → **Check RTV limit**
    > 🟡 **Rule:** *"RTV Max limit is 50 pieces or INR 15,000, whichever is higher (per trip)"*
    - **Decision: "RTV Possible?"**
      - **Yes** → **Initiate RTV to WH**
      - **Not Possible** → **Move to back of shelf until RTV can be done**
- There's also a **"daily check SLA"** governing how often this non-saleable check should run.

**Step 3b — If "No" (nothing non-saleable — the normal path):**
This flows into the **Decision: "Display as per guidelines?"**
- **No** → **Correct sorting & display** → loops back into **Assign category level Placement**
- **Yes** → **Assign category level Placement**

**Step 4 — Assign Category Level Placement**
This is where each product is formally slotted into one of **four display tiers**:
- **Primary** — main counter
- **Secondary** — VM display
- **PDQ** — PP199 (a specific price-point code) only
- **Cash Counter** — PP99 (entry price point) only

> 💡 **From the meeting, in plain terms:** *Primary* is the bulk of NG's stock in the main product zone the Home Center gives them. *Secondary* is extra display space the Home Center's own visual merchandising (VM) team grants at their discretion, wherever they see fit. *PDQ* (a small dedicated impulse-buy box unit) only ever displays the PP199-and-around price band. *Cash Counter* space is not available at every Home Center outlet — only where the store offers a checkout queue-manager display — and only carries the lowest entry price point (previously ₹99, now ₹109).

**Step 5 — Capture Display Photos** *(cadence: Weekly)*
> 🟡 **Sticky note:** *"CCA to store supervisor"* — meaning the front-line staff member submits the photo to their supervisor.

**Step 6 — Decision: "Received photos (Weekly)?"**
- **Yes** → **Supervisor visit check**
- **No** → **Follow-up by AM** → **Action as per picture not received or incorrect VM** → feeds back into **Supervisor visit check**

This closes the loop — display compliance is a continuously monitored cycle, not a one-time setup.

### ✅ Module 3 Quick Recap
- No planogram? → escalate to **NPD/Design team**, get it approved by the **store partner**, then implement.
- Non-saleable stock: **repair-if-possible (prune/clean/shine), else RTV** (capped at 50 pcs or ₹15,000/trip).
- Four placement tiers: **Primary, Secondary, PDQ (PP199), Cash Counter (PP99)**.
- Compliance is proven with a **weekly photo cycle**, escalated by the AM if photos are missing or incorrect.
